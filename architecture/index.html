JustAsh: an evidence-ledger forecasting agent

Twelve earnings figures across four companies, each traceable to a quoted line in a filing.

16 August 2026 Python 3.14 OpenAI gpt-5 BM25 retrieval, no embeddings 62 tests

The system reads a frozen corpus of 1,139 filings, transcripts and slide decks; extracts facts, never forecasts, into an append-only evidence ledger; routes each metric to the estimator its evidence actually supports; and puts every candidate number through a validation gate that ends with a model instructed to prove the number wrong.

Nothing here is asserted by a language model. Every submitted figure is the output of arithmetic whose inputs are quoted, and whose derivation is printed in plain English next to the number.

1. The scoring function is the specification

Before designing anything we read how accuracy is scored, because it determines what "good" means:

metric score = min(5.0, |team miss| / max(|Wall St miss|, floor))
floor        = 0.5pp                      for percentage metrics
             = 0.5% of the reported result for money and EPS metrics

Three properties of that formula shaped every later decision.

The floor decouples us from Wall Street when consensus is good. If consensus is near-perfect the denominator does not collapse toward zero — it is pinned at the floor. An absolute error below the floor therefore scores under 1.0 regardless of how accurate consensus was. The objective is absolute precision, not contrarianism.

Downside is ten times the upside band. The cap is 5.0 while the floor band is 1.0. Eleven metrics at a strong 0.6 plus a single capped 5.0 averages 0.97 — par with consensus. One scale error erases the entire edge, so unit handling is a subsystem with its own tests rather than a helper function.

A missing forecast scores 5.0. No estimator chain may terminate in None. The seasonal-trend estimator exists mainly to guarantee that every metric has a number.

2. The four companies are not the same problem

The most useful thing we found was not in any model — it was in the corpus. The four companies sit in completely different information states, and a single uniform prompt across all four throws that away.

CompanyInformation stateDominant method
Hays
FY2026		 The fiscal year ended 30 June 2026. The 10 July trading update discloses Q4 net-fee growth by division. The period is closed and partly disclosed. Reconstruction
Analog Devices
FY2026 Q3		 The Q2 8-K guides Q3 explicitly: revenue $3.9bn ±$100m, adjusted operating margin 49.0% ±100bps, adjusted EPS $3.30 ±$0.15. Guidance anchor
Home Depot
FY2026 Q2		 Full-year guidance reaffirmed at Q1; the quarter itself is not separately guided. Phasing and build-up
Deere
FY2026 Q3		 Full-year outlook; quarter not separately guided. Segment-level reporting available. Phasing and build-up

For Hays we are not predicting the future. We are reconstructing a period that has already happened. The system reflects that: reconcile.OVERRIDES declares in the open that the build-up wins for Hays net fees, with the reason stated, rather than hiding the preference inside a weight.

A trap we found and defended against. Hays' headline "Group net fees decreased by 5%" is a like-for-like figure, excluding divested operations. The metric we must submit is actual reported net fees, which includes them. The divisional table makes the gap concrete: ANZ was +7% actual but −2% like-for-like. A system that scrapes the headline growth rate and applies it to prior-year fees produces a confidently wrong number. Our component sourcing prompt names this distinction explicitly and marks a component unavailable rather than substituting the wrong basis.

3. Architecture

Two extraction paths, because they fail differently

Retrieval ranking and model attention are both probabilistic. A table row-label match is not. We run both and merge:

  • Deterministic (table_facts.py) parses filing pipe-tables and matches row labels. No model call, no tokens, identical every run.
  • Model (extract.py) transcribes figures from retrieved prose — guidance sentences a table match can never reach.

This was not planned. The first end-to-end run extracted zero facts for ADI's adjusted gross margin, even though the 8-K contains | Adjusted gross margin percentage | 73.0 % | 69.4 % | as a clean row. We had written a table parser and never wired it in. Merging the two paths fixed it and is now a load-bearing design choice.

The ledger enforces analyst discipline as a type constraint

A Fact cannot be constructed without a basis from a closed vocabulary and a non-empty source_quote. Both raise ValueError. The reason is concrete: in the same quarter ADI reported GAAP diluted EPS of $2.40 and adjusted diluted EPS of $3.09 — a 29% gap. Five of our twelve metrics are adjusted or pre-exceptional, and Deere's is explicitly GAAP. A system that conflates them is confidently wrong.

Model-returned facts are additionally checked for fabrication mechanically: the quote must appear in the text we supplied, after whitespace normalisation. Quotes that do not match are dropped and counted in the run log.

Beyond the anchor: post-guidance signals

Guidance anchoring is table stakes. Every team holding the same 8-K will do it, and a forecast equal to consensus scores exactly 1.0 — par. The edge has to come from what moves you off the anchor with evidence.

The frozen corpus contains an asset most uses of it ignore: documents published after the guidance was issued. ADI guided Q3 on 20 May 2026 and the corpus holds a conference transcript from 2 June. Deere's Q2 call was 21 May with an investor presentation on 26 May. Hays' Q4 update was 10 July with filings through 3 August. That window is strictly newer information than the anchor, and it is where management discusses bookings, backlog, channel inventory, pricing and end-market demand — leading indicators of the very quarter we are forecasting.

signals.py reads that window and returns a bounded tilt, not a competing forecast:

guidance midpoint 3900 x (1 + measured historical residual)
                       x (1 + post-guidance signal tilt)   # capped at +/-2.5%

Caps are deliberately tight — 2.5% on revenue, 6% on EPS (which carries operating leverage), 1.0 percentage point on margins — because the scoring function punishes a large miss ten times harder than it rewards a small win. Signals are mean-aggregated rather than summed, so ten weak observations cannot outvote the cap by sheer count, and every signal's quote is verified against the source text exactly as facts are.

It declines to invent signals. On ADI it read a single pricing observation at strength 0.30 and produced a +0.75% tilt. On Deere it returned zero signals and said so: "No genuine post-guidance leading indicators for FY2026Q3 were found in the supplied documents." An estimator that always finds something is not measuring anything.

Where a metric has no guidance at all — Home Depot's and Deere's quarters are not separately guided — the tilt is applied to the seasonal trend instead, since it is then the only forward-looking input available.

The LLM sets named assumptions; Python does the arithmetic

The build-up estimator registers a composition per metric — named components and a formula. The model's only job is to source each component from the ledger and say which fact it used. It is explicitly instructed that a missing component is recoverable and a fabricated one is not.

Nine of the twelve metrics have a registered composition. Two of the remaining three — ADI revenue and adjusted EPS — are directly guided by the company and need none.

# the year is closed; only the second half needs estimating
"HAS:Net fees":
    h1_fy26_net_fees + h2_fy25_net_fees * (1 + h2_fy26_actual_growth/100)

"HAS:Pre-exceptional operating profit":
    h1_fy26_operating_profit + h2_fy25_operating_profit * (1 + h2_fy26_growth/100)

# a real earnings bridge, reported in pence
"HAS:Pre-exceptional basic EPS":
    (operating_profit - net_finance) * (1 - tax_rate/100)
        / weighted_average_basic_shares * 100

# if the reaffirmed full-year guide holds and Q1 is known, the rest is arithmetic
"HD:Comparable sales, total company":
    (4 * fy26_guided_comp_growth - q1_fy26_actual_comp) / 3

"HD:Adjusted diluted EPS":
    fy25_adjusted_eps * (1 + guided_growth/100) * q2_share_of_annual/100

"HD:Net sales":
    prior_year_net_sales * (1 + (comp_growth + non_comp_contribution)/100)

# Deere guides full-year net income, not quarterly EPS
"DE:Diluted EPS (GAAP)":
    fy26_guided_net_income * q3_share_of_annual/100 / diluted_shares

"DE:Production & Precision Ag operating profit":
    segment_net_sales * segment_operating_margin/100

# ADI guides operating margin but not gross margin; add back the opex ratio
"ADI:Adjusted gross margin":
    guided_adj_operating_margin + recent_adj_opex_ratio

The Home Depot comparable-sales composition is the clearest example of using a company's own commitment as a constraint. Home Depot reaffirmed full-year comparable-sales guidance and has reported Q1. If the full year is to land where management says it will, the remaining three quarters must average (4G − Q1) / 3. That is not a prediction about the world; it is arithmetic on a statement the company has publicly committed to.

"The model said 3958" is unreviewable. "H1 actual 464.6 plus H2 prior-year 507.8 grown at −4.0% = 952.1" can be checked line by line by someone who has never seen the code.

4. The validation gate

Three stages, in order, each logged whether or not it finds anything — a log that records only successes cannot evidence that the gate ran.

  1. Scale bands. Every metric carries a plausible range in its own reporting unit. These do double duty: they reject stray cells (a bare column header year is not a Deere segment profit) and they repair scale errors. ADI's 10-Q reports revenue in thousands (3,623,465) where the workbook requires millions — a silent 1000× error worth the capped 5.0.
  2. Cross-metric identities. Implied gross profit cannot exceed revenue; a segment's operating profit cannot exceed the group total; Hays' operating profit cannot exceed its net fees, since profit is fees less costs. Tolerances are deliberately wide — these catch order-of-magnitude and basis errors, not rounding.
  3. Adversarial critic. One model call that sees only the number, its derivation and the identity results, instructed to argue the number is wrong and to default to finding a problem. An objection counts only if it names a specific figure, unit, period or basis that could be checked; vague unease is discarded.

The critic earned its place on its first run, catching three real defects rather than producing plausible noise:

"The proposed 4,018.2 USDm is above management's FY2026 Q3 guidance range of 3,800–4,000 USDm. It overshoots the top end by 18.2 USDm."

"You blended incompatible units. The guidance is $3.30 (USD/share), but you averaged it with a seasonal_trend of −0.119167 that appears to be a percentage."

"The 0% is explicitly a placeholder, not a derived adjusted gross margin. ADI's adjusted gross margin is typically well above 50%."

All three traced to the same root cause, described in section 6.

When the gate cannot resolve a concern it emits the best available estimate with a loud warning. It never blanks a value, because a blank scores 5.0 — the same as being wrong by ten times the floor.

5. Backtest

We cannot measure the thing we are graded on, because the Wall Street benchmark is not visible to us. We can measure the statistic the scoring function is built from: how often our absolute error lands inside the denominator floor. Below the floor, a metric scores under 1.0 whatever consensus did.

python -m avws.backtest replays the identical estimator chain over historical periods whose answers sit in the corpus, passing only facts whose period precedes the target, plus guidance issued for the target period.

Results are inserted here from the measured run before submission. Any figure in this section is copied from .cache/backtest.json; nothing in it is estimated.

Known leakage. Facts are filtered by their own reporting period, not by the publication date of the document containing them. Guidance for the target is published before the target closes, and a prior period's actual is published before the target reports, so the ordering is sound in the ordinary case. The residual leak is restatements: an earlier period revised in a later filing carries the later filing's knowledge. We did not have time to filter on publication date, and we would rather state that than present the number as cleaner than it is.

6. What we got wrong

Recorded as they happened, in docs/build-log.md, rather than reconstructed at the end.

fixedThe seasonal estimator forecast from the wrong anchor

It extrapolated from the latest quarter instead of the same quarter one year earlier, so it carried a Q2 level into a Q3 slot and forecast ADI Q3 revenue at $4,964m against company guidance of $3,900m. Both of the critic's first two objections trace to this. These businesses are strongly seasonal — that is the entire premise of the estimator, and it had been inverted.

fixedA parser written and never connected

tables.py was built, tested against real filings, and then not called from extraction. It cost ADI's adjusted gross margin every fact it should have had. Tests passing on a module says nothing about whether the module is reachable.

fixedSubstring matching turned a cost line into revenue

Row matching used "contains", so a search for Revenue matched Cost of revenues and recorded a cost as a revenue fact. Wrong facts are worse than missing ones because they look authoritative. Matching is now anchored to the start of the label.

fixedPeriod labels compared as raw strings

"FY2026Q3"[:4] is "FY20", not a year. This crashed the first end-to-end run. The crash was the good outcome — the same bug in a comparison would have silently mis-aligned year-on-year growth. Parsing now goes through periods.py, which has its own tests.

fixedThe venue network intercepts TLS

Dependency installs and API calls both failed with certificate errors. One discriminating test — curl reaching the API with ssl_verify_result=0 — showed the network was not blocking anything; it presents a private CA that only the OS trust store carries. truststore.inject_into_ssl() is called at import in config.py. We rejected disabling verification, which would have worked and would have switched off certificate checking for every request carrying our API key.

7. Known weaknesses

  • thinGuidance residuals are calibrated on few observations. Where fewer than three paired guidance-and-outcome periods exist, the residual is set to zero and the estimate reports that in its own derivation string rather than implying a calibration that does not exist.
  • gapOne metric has neither guidance nor a composition. Deere's worldwide net sales and revenues rests on the seasonal path alone. Nine of twelve metrics have a registered build-up and two more (ADI revenue and adjusted EPS) are directly guided, so this is the single weakest number in the set and we would say so before a judge asked.
  • thinSignal tilts are read from management language. Converting qualitative commentary into a numeric strength is the softest inference in the system. It is capped tightly for exactly that reason, and it declines to fire when it finds nothing — Deere returned zero signals rather than manufacturing one.
  • gapHays net fees is not reachable by table matching. Its divisional statement puts the label on a header row with values on the rows beneath, which our row matcher does not follow. That metric depends on the model path and the build-up.
  • noiseTable harvesting still admits some mis-attributed periods. Multi-year series rows are rejected outright, but a comparative column can still be assigned to the wrong period in unusual layouts.
  • scopeBM25 misses paraphrase. A metric described in unusual wording may not be retrieved. Mitigated by filtering to the right document rather than by better ranking.
  • scopeThe critic can err in both directions. It may accept a bad number or object to a good one, which is why a concrete objection is recorded and bounded rather than allowed to drive unlimited re-derivation.
  • blindConsensus is not observable to us. We cannot check our numbers against the benchmark we are scored against, which is precisely why the backtest targets the floor band instead.

8. Deliberate non-choices

Things we considered and rejected, with reasons, since "we did not have time" and "we decided not to" are different claims.

RejectedWhy
Vector embeddings over the corpus Financial retrieval here is literal — "adjusted gross margin", "net fees", "Production & Precision Ag" are exact strings in the filings. Lexical matching finds them, costs no tokens, and is byte-for-byte reproducible between runs.
Live web retrieval The frozen corpus contains the decisive documents, and a local-only run has no network dependency at 17:15 on a venue network we had already watched break TLS.
Multi-agent debate as the architecture Numbers would still be asserted rather than derived, and backtesting would cost N× the tokens. We kept its one good idea — an adversarial challenger — and put it in the gate.
Clamping forecasts into the guidance range Companies do beat the top end; ADI said so in the same release we read guidance from. We fixed the estimator that caused the overshoot instead of capping the symptom.
