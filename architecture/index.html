Note 1

The scoring function is the specification

We read how accuracy is scored before looking at a single company, because it defines what "good" means here.

metric score = min(5.0, |team miss| / max(|Wall St miss|, floor))
floor        = 0.5pp                       percentage metrics
             = 0.5% of the reported result money and EPS metrics

The floor decouples us from Wall Street when consensus is good. If consensus is near-perfect the denominator does not collapse toward zero — it is pinned at the floor. An absolute error inside that floor scores under 1.0 regardless of how accurate consensus was. The objective is absolute precision, not contrarianism.

The downside is ten times the upside band. Eleven metrics at a strong 0.6 plus a single capped 5.0 averages 0.97 — par with consensus. One unit error erases a day of good analysis, which is why unit handling is a module with its own test suite.

A missing forecast scores 5.0. No chain may terminate in None. The seasonal estimator exists largely to guarantee every metric has a number, and the workbook writer refuses to emit an incomplete file rather than writing a blank cell.

Note 2

Four companies, four different problems

The most useful thing we found was in the corpus, not in a model. These four companies sit in completely different information states, and one uniform prompt across all four discards that.

CompanyInformation stateMethod it earns
Hays
FY2026		 The fiscal year ended 30 June 2026. The 10 July trading update discloses Q4 net-fee growth by division. The period is closed and partly disclosed. Reconstruction
Analog Devices
FY2026 Q3		 The Q2 8-K guides the quarter explicitly: revenue $3.9bn ±$100m, adjusted operating margin 49.0% ±100bps, adjusted EPS $3.30 ±$0.15. Guidance anchor
Home Depot
FY2026 Q2		 Full-year guidance reaffirmed at Q1; the quarter itself is not separately guided. Implied-guide arithmetic
Deere
FY2026 Q3		 Full-year outlook only, with segment-level reporting available. Phasing and build-up

A trap we found and defended against. Hays' headline "Group net fees decreased by 5%" is a like-for-like figure, excluding divested operations. The metric we submit is actual reported net fees, which includes them. The divisional table makes the gap concrete: ANZ was +7% actual but −2% like-for-like. A system that scrapes the headline and applies it to prior-year fees produces a confidently wrong number. Our component-sourcing prompt names the distinction and marks a component unavailable rather than substituting the wrong basis.

Note 3

How the system is put together

Orchestration and reproducibility

One command, no human input: python -m avws.run --all. The four companies run concurrently, and each company's three metrics run concurrently up to the cross-metric identity checks, which need all three at once. Ledger and log writes are locked. A full run takes about six minutes, leaving genuine retry room inside the 45-minute final window — a sequential version took ten and left none.

Reproducibility is deliberate. Retrieval is lexical and therefore byte-identical between runs. Model calls run at temperature 0 and every one is archived with its prompt, schema and response. The historical series is cached with the prompt inside the cache key, so editing a prompt invalidates the entry rather than silently serving stale output; --fresh bypasses the cache entirely, which is how the submitted run was produced.

Note 4

Where the information comes from, and how we check it

The supplied corpus of 1,139 filings, call transcripts and slide decks, frozen 14 August 2026. No live web retrieval: the decisive documents are all in the corpus, and a local-only run has no network dependency at 17:15 on a venue network we had already watched intercept TLS.

Three extraction paths, because they fail differently

  • Deterministic row matching parses filing pipe-tables and matches row labels. No model call, no tokens, identical every run. Right for finding build-up components.
  • Model transcription reads retrieved prose — guidance sentences a table match can never reach.
  • Focused series extraction asks one question directly: this exact metric, on this exact basis, for the last N comparable periods.

The third exists because the first two were not enough. Reconstructing a time series from row-label matches kept trading one class of junk for another — labels are ambiguous, columns are ambiguous — and produced Hays EPS at 36 pence against a true level near 1.3. Asking directly, with comparability enforced in code rather than requested in a prompt, fixed it.

Citations are a type constraint, not a convention

A Fact cannot be constructed without a basis from a closed vocabulary and a non-empty source_quote. Both raise. The reason is concrete: in the same quarter ADI reported GAAP diluted EPS of $2.40 and adjusted diluted EPS of $3.09 — a 29% gap. Five of our twelve metrics are adjusted or pre-exceptional; Deere's is explicitly GAAP.

Fabrication is caught mechanically rather than trusted away: a returned quote must appear as a substring of the text we supplied, after whitespace normalisation. Quotes that do not match are dropped and counted in the run log.

Currency and trustworthiness

Every fact passes one normalisation gate carrying a plausible band in the metric's own unit. The band rejects stray cells — a bare column-header year is not a Deere segment profit — and it repairs scale: ADI's 10-Q reports revenue in thousands (3,623,465) where the workbook requires millions. Money metrics rescale; percentages never do, because "repairing" −40% into −0.04% invents a number rather than correcting one.

Note 5

How research becomes a number

Four estimator families produce candidates. The model's role is confined to reading and to setting named assumptions; every arithmetic step is Python, and every step is printed.

Guidance anchor, with measured bias

Where a company guides the period, that is the strongest input available. But companies are systematically biased against their own outlook — ADI's Q2 release says its quarter came in "above the high end of our outlook" — so the anchor carries a residual measured from paired guidance and outcomes across the corpus. Both quotes must verify, and a gap above 60% is treated as a mis-paired basis rather than a guidance miss. Below three pairs it reports zero and says so rather than implying a calibration it does not have.

Beyond the anchor: post-guidance signals

Anchoring alone is table stakes — every team holding the same 8-K will do it, and a forecast equal to consensus scores exactly 1.0. The corpus contains an asset most uses of it ignore: documents published after the guidance was issued. ADI guided Q3 on 20 May and the corpus holds a conference transcript from 2 June; Deere's Q2 call was 21 May with an investor deck on 26 May. That window is strictly newer information, and it is where management discusses bookings, backlog, channel inventory and pricing.

Those signals become a bounded tilt — 2.5% on revenue, 6% on EPS, 1.0pp on margins — mean-aggregated so ten weak observations cannot outvote the cap by count. On ADI it read a single pricing signal and tilted +0.75%. On Deere it returned zero and said so. An estimator that always finds something is not measuring anything.

The signal category that turned out to matter most: explicit positioning against consensus. Companies routinely say where they will land relative to the analyst estimates — "in line with market expectations", "at the top of the range", "ahead of consensus". That is a direct statement about the sign of the surprise, from the only party that already knows, and it arrives as one sentence of prose rather than a number in a table.

We were missing it. Hays said on 10 July that FY26 pre-exceptional operating profit would be at the top of the consensus range, upgrading an earlier in-line statement. Our system had captured the earlier anchor of £45.2m and missed the upgrade — so it was about to submit £44.89m, below the consensus mean it is scored against, on the one metric where the company had publicly signalled the direction of the surprise.

The extractor now treats top-of-range and ahead-of-consensus as strong ABOVE signals, in-line as no signal, and a later statement as superseding an earlier one. It fires three signals on that metric and surfaces the range itself: £37m–£46m.

Build-up identities

Nine of twelve metrics have a registered composition — named components and a formula. The model sources each component from the ledger and says which fact it used; it is told explicitly that a missing component is recoverable and a fabricated one is not.

HD · comparable sales, total company 1.13%
(4 × FY guidance midpoint 1.00% − Q1 actual 0.60%) ÷ 3 remaining quarters = 1.13%
Home Depot reaffirmed full-year comparable-sales guidance and has reported Q1. If the year is to land where management says it will, the remaining three quarters must average what the arithmetic requires. Not a prediction about the world — a constraint the company has publicly committed to.
HAS · pre-exceptional basic EPS 1.29pence
(45.09 operating profit − 13.40 net finance) × (1 − 25% tax) ÷ 1,590.2m basic shares × 100 = 1.29p
Not estimated — derived. See Note 6.
Note 6

Three things a weighted average does not do

EPS is derived, not estimated

Producing twelve numbers independently and checking them afterwards makes consistency checked rather than structural. A sell-side analyst does not estimate EPS; they build one linked model and EPS falls out of it.

Our system demonstrated why that matters. It produced Hays pre-exceptional basic EPS of 36.6 pence alongside pre-exceptional operating profit of £44.5m. With roughly 1,600m shares those cannot both be true. Independent estimation permits the contradiction; a linked model forbids it. linkage.py extracts the drivers once per company — share count, effective tax rate, net finance charge, all quote-verified — derives the metric, and reports both values with the divergence so the disagreement stays visible.

The submitted number optimises the score, not the average

The metric score is linear in absolute error inside the cap, so expected absolute error is minimised at the median of the predictive distribution, not its mean — and the cap makes the loss more robust still. Our candidates disagreed by 111% on ADI adjusted EPS: the mean would have landed in an empty region between two clusters no estimator considers plausible; the median landed on $3.30, the company's own guidance.

We estimate where the crowd is

We are scored against a consensus mean we cannot see. For a guided metric, consensus clusters near the guidance midpoint, because that is the strongest public signal every analyst also holds. Making that proxy explicit turns an accidental deviation into a stated decision; any deviation above 5% is flagged in the run log.

Note 7

Validation, conflicts and failure handling

Three stages, in order, each logged whether or not it finds anything. A log that records only successes cannot evidence that the gate ran.

  1. Scale bands against the metric's plausible range, which also repair thousands-versus-millions errors and reject percentages arriving as decimal fractions.
  2. Cross-metric identities. Implied gross profit cannot exceed revenue; a segment's operating profit cannot exceed the group total; Hays' operating profit cannot exceed its net fees, because profit is fees less costs. Tolerances are deliberately wide — these catch order-of-magnitude and basis errors, not rounding.
  3. Adversarial critic. One model call seeing only the number, its derivation and the identity results, instructed to argue the number is wrong and to default to finding a problem. An objection counts only if it names a specific figure, unit, period or basis that could be checked; vague unease is discarded.

The critic has found more real defects than our test suite. Verbatim:

"That figure is 40 bps (≈−0.40%), not −40%, and it refers to the FX impact component, not the total comparable sales. Using −40 instead of −0.40 drives your average to −25.09% by a two-orders-of-magnitude unit error."

"You mixed periods in the growth input: the −64.9% figure is an H1 2026 year-on-year change, not a full-year one."

Both were real, and neither was caught by a unit test.

Conflicts. Where the same period appears twice with different values — a press release rounds "$3.62 billion" where the statement table gives 3,623 — the more precise reading wins. Where two estimators disagree by more than 10% the disagreement is recorded as a warning on the estimate and surfaced in the evidence report rather than averaged away silently.

Failures. One company failing cannot lose the other three: each runs in its own task and a crash is logged with its traceback rather than converted into an empty result. A failed critic call degrades to "unreviewed" and says so. When the gate cannot resolve a concern it emits the best available estimate with a loud warning — never a blank, because a blank scores 5.0, the same as being wrong by ten times the floor.

Note 9

Backtest

We cannot measure what we are graded on, because the Wall Street benchmark is not visible to us. We can measure the statistic the scoring function is built from: how often our absolute error lands inside the denominator floor. Below the floor, a metric scores under 1.0 whatever consensus did.

python -m avws.backtest replays the estimator chain over historical periods whose answers sit in the corpus, passing only facts whose period precedes the target plus guidance issued for it. These are the measured results, copied from .cache/backtest.json.

We do not claim this measures the system we are submitting, and it would be dishonest to present it that way. It measures the seasonal fallback on sparse extracted history — the weakest path we have. For a historical period there is no guidance fact in the ledger and no sourced build-up components, so the harness never exercises the guidance anchor or the build-up compositions, which between them produce most of the twelve submitted numbers. Read it as evidence about our worst case, not our expected case.

The first version of this harness measured nothing at all. It drew its target values from the whole ledger, including opportunistically row-matched facts, so it scored forecasts against unreliable targets and reported MAE of 31,275 on a metric near 45,000 while the median error was 1,406 — the signature of a few mis-attributed periods rather than a real error distribution. Targets are now restricted to the focused series extraction. A probe that cannot separate a good forecast from a bad one is not evidence, and we would rather publish a poor measured number than a flattering meaningless one.

Known leakage. Facts are filtered by their own reporting period, not by the publication date of the document containing them. Guidance for the target is published before the target closes and a prior period's actual before the target reports, so the ordering is sound in the ordinary case. The residual leak is restatements: an earlier period revised in a later filing carries the later filing's knowledge.

Note 10

What we got wrong

Recorded in docs/build-log.md as they happened rather than reconstructed at the end. Every one of these shipped into a run before it was caught.

fixedThe seasonal estimator forecast from the wrong anchor

It extrapolated from the latest quarter rather than the same quarter a year earlier, carrying a Q2 level into a Q3 slot and forecasting ADI Q3 revenue at $4,964m against guidance of $3,900m. Seasonality is the entire premise of that estimator, and it had been inverted.

fixedA parser written and never connected

tables.py was built, tested against real filings, and then not called from extraction. ADI's adjusted gross margin extracted zero facts despite sitting in the 8-K as a clean table row. Tests passing on a module say nothing about whether the module is reachable.

fixedThe guard ate its own inputs

One plausibility band was doing two jobs. Sized for Hays group net fees near 972 GBPm, it rejected the divisional components the build-up needs — 308.9, 192.2, 116.2 — discarding 8 of 10 extracted facts. Components need a wider band than the figure they compose.

fixedBands guarded one extraction path but not the other

A filing line reading "FX negatively impacted comparable sales by approximately 40 basis points" entered the ledger as −40 percentage points and dragged Home Depot's comparable sales to −25%. The defence existed and half the door was open.

fixedPeriod types pooled together

The estimator mixed full years with quarters and paired non-adjacent years, so a year-to-quarter ratio was used as a growth rate. Deere's segment operating profit came out at 441 against a true level in the low thousands.

fixedA median of two values that was not a median

Our weighted-median walk returns the first candidate crossing cumulative 0.5 — which with two candidates is simply the heavier one. It discarded exactly the disagreement the decision layer exists to handle. The median of two values is their midpoint.

revertedModel tiering, measured and rejected

We hypothesised that extraction is transcription and would run correctly on a smaller model. Measured: 40% faster and materially less accurate. Deciding whether "£45.6m" is a half year or a full year, or whether a figure is the quarter or the year-to-date cumulative, is exactly the judgement that makes a series right. Hays half-years were labelled as full years and ADI revenue came out at $71bn against $3.9bn of guidance. The switch is kept so the experiment stays reproducible rather than merely asserted.

Note 11

Known weaknesses

  • weakDeere's worldwide net sales is our weakest number. No company guidance for the quarter and the thinnest composition of the twelve. We would say so before a judge asked.
  • thinCalibration samples are small. Most metrics yielded fewer than three verified guidance-and-outcome pairs, so their residual is zero by rule. Only Hays operating profit reached a measured bias — +0.51% across 3 pairs, 100% beat rate.
  • thinSignal tilts read management language. Turning qualitative commentary into a numeric strength is the softest inference in the system, which is why it is capped tightly and declines to fire when it finds nothing.
  • gapNo three-statement linkage beyond EPS. A real model derives every line from shared drivers. We derive one metric and check the rest.
  • gapNo consensus distribution. We proxy the mean but model neither its dispersion nor analyst revision breadth, both of which a fund would weigh heavily.
  • scopeBM25 misses paraphrase. Mitigated by filtering to the right document rather than by better ranking.

Where the system stops being general

Roughly 80% of the code is company-agnostic: corpus, tables, units, periods, ledger, signals, guidance, seasonal, reconcile, decide, workbook, and a registry that reads companies.json — so new companies and metrics need no code at all. Adding a fifth company needs about four small config entries: a row-label alias, a plausible band, an optional composition formula, and optional retrieval hints. The clearest next improvement is deriving bands from extracted history instead of declaring them.
